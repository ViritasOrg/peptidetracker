# Female Enhancement Protocols — Scientific Research Foundation

Source: PubMed / PMC / Lancet / FDA label searches conducted 2026-06-14.
Purpose: Evidence base for female-specific compound protocols in the app.

**IMPORTANT NOTE ON EVIDENCE QUALITY:** Research on AAS/enhancement compounds in women is substantially thinner than in men. Most AAS clinical trials enrolled male subjects only. Female-specific data comes from:
1. Medical use trials (burn injury, cancer cachexia, HIV wasting, HSDD)
2. Qualitative studies of female AAS users
3. Case reports documenting virilization
4. Community/practitioner guides (not peer-reviewed — labelled as such below)

Features for female users must present evidence levels honestly and flag where data is community-derived rather than clinical.

## 1. Testosterone Replacement in Women

### Key Paper — Lancet 2019 Meta-Analysis

**Islam RM et al. "Safety and efficacy of testosterone for women: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trial data."**
*Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol. 2019;7(10):754-766. DOI: 10.1016/S2213-8587(19)30189-5*

- 36 RCTs, 8,480 women
- **Efficacy:** Testosterone significantly improved sexual function (desire, arousal, orgasm, pleasure) in postmenopausal women
- **Main indication:** Hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)
- **Androgenic side effects:** Higher rate of non-scalp hair growth vs. placebo; no significant increase in cardiovascular events or breast cancer at physiologic doses
- **Route preference:** Transdermal strongly preferred — avoids hepatic first-pass, maintains physiologic serum levels, lower virilization risk than injectable
- **No FDA-approved formulation exists for women** — all use is off-label or compounded

### Global Consensus Position Statement (2019)
*Endorsed by International Menopause Society, Endocrine Society, NAMS, ISSWSH, and 10 other organisations*

- Recommends testosterone for postmenopausal women with HSDD
- **Target range:** Serum testosterone at or below upper limit of normal female reference range (typically total T ~0.5–2.5 nmol/L)
- **Do not exceed** male physiologic range — risk of irreversible virilization increases sharply
- Monitor for androgenic symptoms at every follow-up

### Clinical Dosing — Female Testosterone

| Route | Dose range | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Transdermal (patch/gel) | ~300 mcg/day | Physiologic; lowest virilization risk |
| Compounded cream | 0.5–2mg/day | Widely used; absorption variable |
| IM injection (enanthate) | 3–25mg/week | RCT range tested; weekly injections cause peak-to-trough swings; higher virilization risk than transdermal |
| Pellet implant | 75–150mg q3-6 months | Poor dose control; over-delivery reported |

**⚠️ Key risk:** Injectable testosterone in women causes the same peak-driven effects as in men (Hct elevation, higher androgen peaks) but at much lower absolute doses. Women are far more sensitive to androgenic effects than men — a dose that is "low" for a man can be supraphysiological for a woman.

## 2. Oxandrolone (Anavar) — Most Clinically Studied AAS in Women

### Clinical Evidence Summary

Oxandrolone is the most extensively studied anabolic steroid in female clinical populations, used across burn injury, HIV wasting, Turner syndrome, and surgical recovery trials.

**FDA-evaluated doses in women:**
- 5mg twice daily (10mg/day) — primary clinical dose
- 6mg twice daily (12mg/day) — rotator cuff trial (NCT03091075)
- Trials lasting >6 months at these doses: no androgenic effects, hirsutism, acne, or behavioural changes reported

**PMID references:**
- PMID 9253907 — Oxandrolone significantly increases weight gain in burn recovery
- PMID 10757193 — Anticatabolic and wound healing effects in severe burn injury
- PMC9020845 — RCT in severe burn patients (mixed sex)
- ScienceDirect 2025 — Systematic review: safety and effectiveness of oxandrolone across clinical conditions

### Virilization Risk at Clinical Doses

| Effect | Reversibility | Dose/Duration threshold |
|--------|--------------|------------------------|
| Menstrual irregularities | Usually reversible | Low doses, short duration |
| Acne, oily skin | Reversible | Dose-dependent |
| Hirsutism (body hair) | Partially reversible | Duration-dependent |
| Clitoromegaly | **Often irreversible** | Any dose if prolonged |
| Voice deepening | **Irreversible** | Any dose — no safe threshold confirmed |
| Alopecia (scalp) | Partially reversible | Dose-dependent |

**⚠️ Critical:** FDA label states explicitly: *"Some virilizing changes in women are irreversible even after prompt discontinuance of therapy and are not prevented by concomitant use of estrogens."* This applies to voice and clitoromegaly in particular.

### Community-Derived Female Dosing (Not RCT-validated)
- Competitive female athletes: 5–20mg/day, 4–8 week cycles
- Conservative/first use: 5mg/day for 4–6 weeks
- **Cycles kept short specifically to reduce irreversible virilization risk**

## 3. Voice Change — Irreversibility Evidence

**PMID 40641310** — "Voice Change as a Result of Androgen Supplementation in Cisgender Women: A Scoping Review" (2026)
- Systematic review of voice changes in women receiving androgen therapy
- **Key finding:** Voice changes are **dose- and duration-dependent** but irreversibility is documented across routes and compounds
- Intramuscular injectable testosterone associated with greater vocal pitch lowering and voice instability than transdermal
- **No established safe dose threshold** to guarantee prevention of voice change
- Voice masculinisation reported as permanent even after discontinuation

**Case report — Bensoussan et al. (2019), Clinical Case Reports (Wiley)**
- 20-year follow-up: voice changes from AAS persisted permanently
- Vocal changes documented on laryngoscopy: thickened vocal folds, lowered fundamental frequency

**⚠️ Feature implication:** Voice change is an irreversible adverse effect with no established safe dose floor. The app should surface this prominently for female users logging AAS compounds.

## 4. Primobolan (Metenolone) in Women

### Evidence Status: LOW — Community-Derived Only

No rigorous RCT has studied metenolone specifically in women for enhancement purposes.

**Pharmacological basis for lower virilization risk:**
- DHT derivative → does not aromatize to estrogen
- Does not 5α-reduce to more potent androgens in target tissues
- Lower androgenic:anabolic ratio than testosterone
- No 17α-alkylation (oral acetate form is 1-methylated) → lower hepatotoxicity

**Community-derived dosing for women** (not clinically validated):
- Oral metenolone acetate: 10–25mg/day (low end); 25–50mg/day (moderate)
- Injectable metenolone enanthate: 25–75mg/week
- Cycle duration: 4–8 weeks typically cited

**⚠️ Caveat:** "Low virilization" is relative. Primobolan still carries all the risks in the table above (clitoromegaly, voice change). "Safer" does not mean "safe." No clinical trial data validates these doses for women.

## 5. Nandrolone (NPP / Deca) in Women

### Pharmacological Basis

Nandrolone's unique 5α-reductase pathway (reduces to dihydronandrolone = near-zero androgenic activity) makes it theoretically less virilising than testosterone in scalp/skin tissues, while retaining anabolic activity in muscle.

**However:** Nandrolone has progestogenic activity (binds progesterone receptor), which in women causes:
- Menstrual disruption
- Potential mood effects
- Interaction with female hormonal cycle

**Community-derived dosing for women** (not clinically validated):
- NPP preferred over Deca due to shorter ester (faster clearance if side effects emerge)
- Typical range cited: 25–50mg/week
- Cycle: 6–8 weeks maximum

**⚠️ Evidence gap:** No clinical trials specifically studying nandrolone in women for enhancement purposes. All female dosing data is community-derived.

## 6. SARMs in Women — Clinical Evidence

### Ostarine (Enobosarm) — Best-Studied SARM in Women

**PMID 23499390** — "Effects of enobosarm on muscle wasting: double-blind, randomised controlled phase 2 trial" (Lancet Oncol, 2013)
- Included postmenopausal women with cancer cachexia
- Doses: 1mg/day and 3mg/day orally
- Results: Dose-dependent lean mass preservation; no virilization reported at these doses
- These are low therapeutic doses, not enhancement doses

**PMID 27138015 / PMC4853438** — POWER trial design (Phase 3, 2016)
- Enobosarm 3mg/day × 147 days in mixed male/female cancer patients
- Female patients: postmenopausal — no virilization signal reported at 3mg/day

**Community enhancement dosing for women** (not clinically validated):
- 5–12.5mg/day cited in non-clinical sources
- **At enhancement doses, tissue selectivity decreases** — androgenic effects emerge
- No dose-response data for virilization at enhancement doses in women

### GSK2881078 (Unnamed SARM) — Clinical Data in Women

**JCEM 2018 — "GSK2881078 produces dose-dependent increases in lean mass in healthy older men and women"**
*J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2018;103(9):3215-3224*
- RCT including healthy older women
- Doses: 0.5mg and 2mg/day
- Lean mass increased dose-dependently in women
- No significant virilization at these doses in this short-term trial

### LGD-4033 (Ligandrol)

**⚠️ No published clinical trial data in women.** All dosing recommendations for women using LGD-4033 are anecdotal. Community-cited doses of 2mg/day are not clinically validated. Liver toxicity cases documented (PMC9647117 — see Doc 06).

## 7. Female AAS Pharmacokinetics — Sex Differences

**PMC11653648** — "Impacts of AAS supplementation on female health and offspring: Mechanisms, side effects, and medical perspectives" (Saudi Pharmaceutical Journal, 2024)

Key sex-based pharmacokinetic differences:
- Women have **lower baseline androgen levels** → the relative increase from the same dose is far greater
- Women have **less SHBG** on average during some cycle phases → higher free androgen availability per mg
- Women metabolise androgens differently (different P450 enzyme expression profiles)
- **Result:** Women should use approximately 1/10th to 1/5th of male doses to achieve equivalent physiologic effect; supraphysiological doses in women are reached at far lower absolute amounts

### AAS Effects on Female Reproductive Health

- HPG axis suppression: AAS inhibit GnRH pulsatility → LH/FSH suppression → anovulation and amenorrhoea
- Menstrual disruption: common even at low doses; usually reversible on cessation
- Fertility: AAS use during reproductive years carries risk of anovulatory infertility; recovery is typically but not universally achieved after cessation
- Pregnancy risk: AAS are teratogenic — use during pregnancy is contraindicated absolutely

## 8. Harm Reduction Framework for Female Users

### Compound Safety Ranking (Least → Most Virilising)

| Rank | Compound | Evidence basis |
|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Testosterone transdermal (physiologic TRT) | Strong (RCT) |
| 2 | Oxandrolone 5–10mg/day, <8 weeks | Moderate (FDA clinical trials) |
| 3 | Ostarine 1–3mg/day | Moderate (Phase 2/3 cancer trials) |
| 4 | Primobolan oral 10–25mg/day | Weak (community-derived) |
| 5 | Nandrolone (NPP) 25–50mg/week | Weak (community-derived) |
| 6 | Testosterone injectable (supraphysiological) | High virilization risk |
| 7 | Trenbolone | **Avoid — extremely high virilization risk** |

### Non-Negotiable Safety Rules for Female Users

1. **Stop at first sign of voice change** — this effect is irreversible once established; no dose is confirmed safe
2. **Clitoromegaly monitoring** — document at baseline; cessation required at any change
3. **Short cycles only** — 4–8 weeks maximum for any androgenic compound; virilization risk is duration-dependent
4. **Pregnancy status** — confirm not pregnant before any AAS/enhancement use; all these compounds are teratogenic
5. **Menstrual cycle tracking** — disruption is an early warning sign of HPG axis suppression
6. **Lower doses always** — start at 1/10th of male dose; female androgen sensitivity is profoundly greater

### Bloodwork Additions for Women

Beyond the standard panel (Doc 06), female users should additionally monitor:
- **LH / FSH** — HPG axis suppression indicator
- **Estradiol (E2)** — androgen aromatisation and cycle disruption marker
- **Progesterone** — especially relevant with progestogenic compounds (nandrolone, trenbolone)
- **Prolactin** — may be elevated with progestogenic AAS
- Baseline menstrual cycle documentation (length, regularity) before starting any protocol

## 9. Feature Design Constraints — Female Users

1. **Compound risk flag by sex** — each compound should carry a sex-specific virilization risk label (none/low/moderate/high)
2. **Voice change warning** — must appear for any androgenic compound logged by a female user; irreversibility must be stated
3. **Dose guard** — if female user logs a testosterone dose in injection range, surface comparison to male TRT doses to contextualise the androgen load
4. **Cycle length limit nudge** — flag at 6 weeks for any androgenic compound for female users (vs. 12 weeks for males)
5. **Pregnancy disclaimer** — female users should be prompted to confirm non-pregnancy status when logging AAS compounds
6. **Menstrual disruption prompt** — include in periodic check-in if female user is not on continuous hormonal contraception
7. **Evidence level display** — make clear when a dose is community-derived vs. clinically validated; Primobolan and nandrolone doses for women are not RCT-backed
